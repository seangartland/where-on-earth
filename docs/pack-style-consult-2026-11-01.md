# Pack word and Passport header: one text style (2026-11-01)

Method: Opus 5.5 (Claude Code), judgment only, nothing was built or rendered. Inputs: the 390 px header mock `docs/passport-header-mock-2026-11-01.png` (variants A, B, C), the three live pack arts in `assets/`, and `docs/pack-accent-consult-2026-11-01.md`. Font proportions below are estimated from the pack images, not measured. I could not tell from the repo whether the current pack words are generated by the image model or composited, so that is treated as unknown.

## Verdict: Direction 3, one letterform with two renderings

Sean is right that there are too many styles today, but the main problem is not flat vs gold. It is that the pack word and the header use **different letterforms**. The pack word is a heavy condensed grotesque with a chrome bevel. Mock B is a wide system sans. Mock C copies the bevel. Same caps, different shapes, so they read as two brands whatever the color.

The fix is one typeface, one case, one tracking rule, and one color logic everywhere. The word is then rendered two ways, the way any brand mark has a full-color version and a one-color version:

- **On a photo (pack art):** gold fill, pack ink edge, pack glow. These three are kept because they do a job: they make the word readable over any photo, and they match the satin gold wrapper.
- **On paper (Passport):** the same word in solid pack ink. That is the pack word's own edge color, printed flat, the way a stamp prints in one ink. That fits the Passport's paper and stamp style.

What goes: the 3D bevel, the extrusion and the chrome highlights. They are the most decorative part, they are the "generated" look, and nothing else in the game uses them. Once they are gone, the pack word and the header are visibly the same mark.

Color system, closed and the same on both surfaces:
- **Gold** = the product line (wrapper, seal, band, pack word fill). One gold for all packs.
- **Ink** = the pack name (pack word edge, header text).
- **Glow** = the pack accent (pack word halo, header bar, progress fill).

This replaces the earlier consult's per-pack gold tints (rose gold, champagne). Those added a third variable for very little gain. The per-pack ink and glow colors stay as they were.

## Why the others lose

**Direction 1 (C-style gold headers)** loses on legibility and taste. The mock shows it: gold on cream is about 2:1 contrast, the glow turns to a colored smudge at 30 px, and three of them stacked look like WordArt. The headers would also become louder than the stamps they introduce, and a fourth style would enter the Passport's paper language. It is the cheapest option (CSS only) but the weakest result.

**Direction 2 (flat packs)** loses on the pack screen, where the bare pack art is the interface. A flat one-color word over a photo either needs a backing plate (one more element) or fails on some scenes: dark ink vanishes on the Tokyo night city and fights the orange Delicate Arch rock. A flat label on a photoreal foil wrapper also looks like a UI sticker pasted onto the product and breaks the collectible illusion. What makes the pack word premium and readable is gold, edge and glow, not the bevel. Direction 3 keeps those three and drops only the bevel.

## Shared spec

**Typeface (both surfaces):** Oswald 700 (SIL OFL), uppercase, self-hosted as a caps-only subset (A to Z, a few kilobytes). A condensed heavy grotesque is the closest open match to the current pack proportions. A wide face such as Inter Black would need much smaller caps to fit LANDMARKS across the pack. The narrow width also helps the 390 px header row. Words are the wrapper words: CAPITALS, LANDMARKS, LANDFORMS.

**Per-pack colors (unchanged from the accent consult):**

| Pack | Ink | Glow |
|---|---|---|
| CAPITALS | `#0B2A6B` | `#3FA9FF` |
| LANDMARKS | `#5A1420` | `#FF6B57` |
| LANDFORMS | `#0F3D33` | `#3ED9A0` |

### Pack art (900 x 1350)

- Composite the word with a script from the Oswald font file onto word-free pack art. Do not generate it with the image model. The letterform then matches the CSS exactly, every pack is identical in treatment, and future packs cost one script run.
- Size: one font size for all three packs, set so the longest word (LANDMARKS) is 540 px wide. Centered at x = 450, baseline at y = 1090 (the current position). Tracking +0.02em.
- Fill: vertical linear gradient `#FFE9A6` 0%, `#E8B53A` 55%, `#B07F22` 100%. Smooth, with no bevel, specular streaks, texture or extrusion.
- Edge: 6 px outside stroke, solid pack ink.
- Glow: one outer glow, pack glow color, 45% opacity, 20 px blur, no spread.
- Drop shadow: 0 4 px 10 px `rgba(0,0,0,.35)` to lift the word off the photo.
- Nothing else: no second outline, no inner highlight, no 3D depth.

### Passport header (390 px, CSS)

- Layout as in the earlier consult: grid `4px minmax(0,1fr) auto 12px`, min height 48 px, full-width button.
- Word: Oswald 700, uppercase, 18 px, letter spacing `0.05em`, solid pack ink. No gold, gradient, glow, stroke or shadow. All three inks pass AA on the cream paper.
- Accent bar: 4 px, full height, pack glow color.
- Progress: 3 px bar on the bottom edge, track `rgba(52,72,96,.12)`, fill in pack glow color to `collected / total`.
- Count, chevron, and the Everything Else envelope icon: unchanged. The count stays in the system font so the numbers stay tabular.

## Cost and order

- Headers: CSS plus one small font file. Cheap.
- Packs: all three arts get redone. The accent consult already required redoing the words, so most of this cost was already planned. If word-free base art does not exist, the three bases have to be regenerated or inpainted without text first. I don't know which applies.
- Before redoing all three, run a pilot: composite the word on one pack (LANDMARKS, the hardest background) and render the header row at 390 px next to it. Confirm Oswald reads as "the same word" at both sizes, then scale to all three.
