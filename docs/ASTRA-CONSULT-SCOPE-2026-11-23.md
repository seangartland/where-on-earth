## 1. Repair unit, batching, and priority

### Correct the scope arithmetic before planning

The CSVs reproduce 736 main-set flags and 99 pilot flags. Main-set flags are z4: 11, z5: 35, z6: 51, z7: 103, z8: 196, z9: 340. Thus 674 flags are at played levels [5,7,8,9]; 51 are at inactive z6, 11 are lower-zoom evidence, and 99 are pilot z10. These distinctions matter to delivery priority and dependency accounting; they do not make the inactive-level evidence irrelevant to diagnosis.

The 133 cluster rows are **set-qualified cells**, not 133 distinct geographic source windows. Deduplicating the CSV's anchored z5 coordinates yields 127 geographic cells. Furthermore, each z4 flag was assigned only to the northwest z5 cell of its four-cell footprint. Expand that footprint before spatial joins; neither 127 nor 133 is a final count of affected geographic cells. A failed tile at several zooms may describe the same ground defect, so tile counts also overstate independent defect events.

`133 × 337 = 44,821` is valid illustrative arithmetic for full z5/z7/z8/z9 coverage per cluster row. At the earlier historical mean of 10,900 bytes/JPEG it is **488,548,900 bytes**, not a complete-candidate estimate. It duplicates some geographic coverage, omits support, base/lower levels, pages and QA, and excludes z10. A fully populated z10 adds 1,024 positions per z5 cell; generating those everywhere would be unjustified because the pilot is sparse and distinct. Existing coverage is sparse at other levels too: a geometric position count is not an inventory of existing keys or permission to extend coverage.

### Selected repair policy

After confirming defects, group them by connected ground footprint and compatible source/tone decision. Include enough surrounding valid imagery for source selection, filtering and boundary comparison. Start with the previous one-z5-cell comparison halo where applicable; the halo supplies evidence and support, not an obligation to replace every descendant. Extend by one ring when the defect reaches the outer comparison boundary, recording evidence, wrap/clip behavior, ownership and revised bytes. Do not bridge unrelated clean regions merely because they share a z5 bucket or hue.

For each group, resolve one canonical repaired source composite and generate the **union of all affected dependencies**, including base/lower-zoom pixels, intersecting active tiles, filter support and gutters, pages, manifests, hashes and comparison assets. Regenerate each shared asset once. Preserve unchanged source outside the contribution mask. Neighboring repair groups need deterministic ownership and compatible joins; independently exporting the same parent or page and overwriting it is unacceptable.

A selective tile or zoom-limited repair is defensible in two cases:

- A demonstrated derivative-specific bug affects only one export level or pilot product, while the source and other levels pass the same-footprint checks. Rebuild that derivative and its downstream dependencies from valid source.
- A bounded source correction affects a small footprint. Publish only keys in its measured dependency closure, with the source footprint, support and unaffected outputs explicitly accounted for. A passing coarse tile threshold alone does not prove that its pixels are unaffected.

A source correction normally propagates across levels, even where a tile's defect fraction falls below threshold. Omission requires evidence that an asset does not depend on the corrected pixels, or verified output equivalence under the pinned pipeline. Do not patch finished JPEG colors, choose quarters independently per tile, or stop at the failed key list. Where historical source attribution is missing, reconstruct the bounded source footprint with new provenance; do not fabricate the old chain. If a clean partial reconstruction cannot be established, enlarge to a coherent window rather than pretend a sparse patch is valid.

Preserve the live ladder [5,7,8,9] and leave stored z6 unchanged under the existing decision. Record z6 flags as diagnostic evidence and explicitly outside delivery. Keep pilot z10 separate in manifests and coverage; do not promote it into the live ladder. If an actual dependency unexpectedly requires changing z6, revise the plan explicitly before execution.

Each rebuilt output must trace to source IDs/quarter/date, actual paths and checksums, validity and contribution masks, selection/fallback reasons, tone parameters, alignment/filtering, code/config versions and output hash/bytes. Record unchanged inputs by identity without invented provenance. Require complete key/dependency accounting, coverage validity, before/after interior and boundary comparisons at wide/base, z5, z7, z8, z9 and applicable z10, and no new visible seams or color regressions. The earlier polar edge statistics remain polar controls; they are not validated color-defect thresholds.

### Batches and ordering

Use a small representative diagnostic cohort first, then one independently reviewable batch of confirmed, coherent groups. Choose batch size from measured complete output bytes and QA capacity, not a fixed cluster count. Keep the complete candidate at or below **1,000,000,000 bytes**, including tiles, pages, base, manifests, provenance, QA and comparisons. Any combined release must fit that same limit; splitting its prefixes does not create multiple allowances.

The supplied **4,265,874,688-byte projected retained total already includes one 1 GB candidate allowance** above the earlier measured 3,265,874,688 bytes. One additional full 1 GB discoloration candidate would project 5,265,874,688 bytes, if the first allowance remains retained and nothing else changes. It is not today's measured occupancy. Retained iterations, rollback copies and parallel candidate assets accumulate. Refresh the complete object census before upload, count actual uploaded copies once each, and abort if a candidate exceeds its limit or retained storage reaches **10,000,000,000 bytes**. Preserve the earlier projection approval and separate upload authorization; no deletion is implied. Cache/scratch capacity is a separate local planning check. Do not spend nominal headroom on an unmeasured global rebuild.

Prioritize confirmed visible harm at played zooms, then severity and geographic extent, then affected play locations where usage evidence exists, with cost and source coherence as batching considerations. Do not invent traffic weights. Worst fraction alone favors large solid artifacts; largest cluster alone favors repeated zoom coverage and diffuse tint.

The initial diagnostic cohort should include:

| Location / set | Why inspect first |
| --- | --- |
| Pilot z10 512/388, cluster 16,12 | Reported 98.2% red-dominated artifact; strong positive control and Algiers reconciliation anchor. |
| Main 9,13 | Worst fraction 33.7%; strong candidate for early played-zoom repair after visual confirmation. |
| Pilot 17,16 | Worst fraction 31.1%, Congo footprint; geographically distinct severe pilot control. |

Include matched clean and borderline controls in each cohort. This is an inspection order, not a claim that all listed tiles are bad. After calibration, favor confirmed main-set played-zoom defects in the first shipping batch; the severe Algiers/Congo pilot repairs may form a small independent candidate if they do not gate live content. A shared diagnosed cause can justify batching nonadjacent footprints with the same fix, but each retains its own join QA and source ownership.

## 2. Pin the gate before a discoloration build

Treat the present detector as a screening tool. The report's assertion that these saturated hues have no natural Earth-imagery source is too strong: hue and saturation alone cannot distinguish legitimate terrain/water/land-cover color from corruption. Conversely, excluding all blue/green can miss genuine defects. Explicit ocean fill should be evaluated using its known mask and expected color, not by exempting every blue pixel. Check fill correctness and missing land separately. Zero W1/W2 color flags does not clear their seam/fill failures.

Require the following calibration artifact before building discoloration repairs:

1. **Pin the evaluated bytes and population.** Save set/key/zoom/dimensions/content hash, pipeline version and masks. Equal per-zoom counts do not prove staging is byte-identical to live R2. Establish content identity with a checksum-backed manifest for the target snapshot, or label the result staging-only until verified. Do the same for the historical pilot snapshot if available. The pilot scratch images were deleted; rescans need an identified read-only copy/fetch with a pinned manifest, not an assumption that current bytes equal the old audit.
2. **Recover the Algiers evidence once, boundedly.** Search the zfill work, audit command/config/code revision, environment and per-key results for the actual 206-failure run. The available RGB red/blue speckle gate is a separate algorithm, not evidence of an HSV implementation. Recover decoder/color conversion, hue units/endpoints, saturation and value floors, masks, denominator, rounding and strictness of the tile cutoff. Run the recovered test on identical bytes if possible. If the implementation or original bytes cannot be recovered, record that limitation and establish a new versioned baseline; do not reverse-engineer thresholds merely to hit 206.
3. **Reconcile by key, not count.** Produce intersections and differences among historical 206 (if keys exist), census all-hue 610, census defect-hue 99 and the proposed detector. Attribute disagreements to input version, algorithm, masks or confirmed labeling errors. With only the historical aggregate, an exact reconciliation is impossible; say so. Audit examples from both directions of disagreement.
4. **Freeze the numeric implementation.** The inspected script uses Pillow's 8-bit HSV, `S > int(0.7*255)` (therefore S >= 179), and `H*360/255`. Its red/yellow/cyan/magenta bands leave 15–45 degrees out, use half-open endpoints, and impose no value floor. These details must be intentional and tested at boundaries. Record the tile failure comparison as fraction >0.003, not 0.3. Preserve exact pixel counts: the exported fractions are rounded, and some band splits were serialized to four decimal places. Near-threshold decisions should be recomputed from pixels, not reconstructed from rounded CSV summaries. A changed value floor, mask or band partition requires a pixel rescan.
5. **Label images before choosing cutoffs.** Review aligned tile-plus-neighbor and source context, stratified across hue, set, zoom, fraction, land/coast/ocean/fill, plus all severe outliers, borderline cases on both sides, disagreement cases and random apparent negatives. Include the photographed Spain region once located, Algiers, Congo and polar controls. Assign confirmed artifact, legitimate color/fill, or unresolved; record reviewer and reason. Inspect visible bands below the saturation threshold as well. Hold out a geographic subset when tuning, so repeated zooms of one defect do not masquerade as independent validation.
6. **Publish the detector and adjudicated repair list separately.** Use hue-specific S/V/fraction criteria only if the labels justify them; do not invent numeric thresholds now. Report precision/recall and false-positive/false-negative counts by hue and zoom on the labeled sample, alongside its size and limitations. Every selected repair must have confirmed visual/source evidence; unresolved flags remain in the investigation queue. Record reviewed false positives and negative sampling, version the script/config/labels, and rescan the pinned population. A sampled false-negative check does not prove global completeness.

The resulting defect list is an adjudicated, versioned list with stated coverage, not a claim that one automatic saturation test proves the world clean. Require the known positive controls to be caught or explicitly covered by an additional visual/source test. Final acceptance still needs human comparison and no-regression QA; reducing the detector count alone is insufficient.

## 3. Root cause: measure the first stage that goes wrong

A systematic cause is plausible, but unrelated regions sharing a hue is not causal evidence. Cyan/yellow/red/magenta can arise at different stages. The census contains finished-image statistics, not the stage-wise evidence needed to choose between source quality, quarter selection, masking, normalization and export. The main set has no reported saturated-magenta component while the pilot does; investigate the pipeline/version difference as well as shared code.

Before expensive reconstruction, use cached inputs to trace representative positive and matched negative footprints through: available source observations and all four untoned quarters; selected untoned mosaic with validity/observation and selection masks; tone/normalization output; fill/mask/composite output in the actual execution order; resampled lossless derivative; encoded tile; and page. Missing intermediate caches may be recreated only as bounded diagnostic crops, with versions and parameters recorded. This is diagnosis before a repair build, not permission for broad reruns. If historical selection records do not exist, compare reproducible candidate paths without claiming to have recovered historical attribution.

At each stage measure per-band quantiles and extrema, clipping/nonfinite/nodata counts, channel ratios, HSV S/V distributions and spatial artifact masks. Compare the same ground coordinates and valid pixel support. Overlay artifact onset with scene/granule and quarter boundaries, observation counts, selection changes, fill masks, processing chunks and tile edges. Tabulate associations by scene/quarter and pipeline version, using matched clean controls so geography or legitimate land cover does not create a false correlation.

| Observation / controlled comparison | Repair implication |
| --- | --- |
| Artifact already present in one untoned quarter; coherent valid alternatives are clean | Source/quarter selection is a supported hypothesis. Reuse the four-quarter machinery and validity/fallback rules, then verify joins. |
| Selected untoned pixels are clean; artifact first appears after tone/normalization | Hold selection fixed, bypass or correct that step on a diagnostic crop. Check band order, scale/offset, nodata participation, arithmetic range and clipping. Quarter reselection is not an adequate fix for a reproducible transform defect. |
| Artifact tracks fill or validity boundaries and first appears at compositing/resampling | Inspect mask alignment, nodata handling and filtering support; repair the demonstrated failure before considering new imagery. |
| Source composite is clean; only a specific derivative/encoding/page stage is wrong | Correct that stage, regenerate its dependency closure, and verify other levels at the same footprint. Zoom-limited delivery can then be justified. |
| No cached quarter supplies acceptable observations | Retain the earlier sourced-fallback/no-data approval rules; do not substitute ocean for missing land. |

Run two controlled comparisons where inputs permit: fixed untoned source with current versus corrected/bypassed tone, and alternative coherent quarters with the same tone path. A change that removes the artifact without damaging valid controls or joins supports the corresponding cause. A good-looking result after changing both selection and tone does not isolate either cause. Multiple causes may require separate cohorts.

The W1/W2 planner, cache access, ownership, provenance and derivative-generation machinery are reusable infrastructure. Its **quarter-reselection policy is a conditional treatment**, not the diagnosis for all discoloration. A shared transform bug should be corrected once in code, with bounded candidates and regression controls; its repair scope is the measured affected population, potentially including currently unflagged tiles, not the census threshold list. Preserve cached raw data and the earlier zero-metered-rerun rules. New acquisition remains conditional on genuinely absent required source objects; Process API remains prohibited.

## 4. Sequencing, release identity, and W3

Proceed with W1/W2 independently under the approved overlap ownership, four-quarter comparison, fallback, source/derivative QA and storage gates. Add the calibrated color check as regression evidence when ready; do not wait for a worldwide color repair list to prepare the polar build. If diagnosis demonstrates that its selected tone/mask pipeline contains the same bug, fix and revalidate the affected polar outputs before acceptance. Independence is not permission to ship a known shared defect.

Default to **separate versioned discoloration candidates** under the same complete-candidate and retained-storage discipline. This isolates diagnosis, review and rollback from the approved polar scope. Build no assumption that each candidate replaces the previous storage allowance. Pin each candidate to a base source/manifest version and explicit replacement keys/dependencies. Before integration, resolve overlapping source footprints and regenerate shared base/page/manifests from the combined canonical source state. Rebase and revalidate a candidate made against an older state; never promote its stale shared assets over newer repairs. For a combined release, account for the complete integrated artifact set and all retained intermediate candidates.

Polar staging and review can finish first. A later separately authorized scoped release is not equivalent to final-map acceptance. The earlier requirement that repaired sources and production pages converge on the same accepted candidate remains, and the census does not waive unresolved visible source defects or Sean's phone/final-map approval. No flag removal follows from either the polar pass or a lower color-failure count.

**W3 changes from the presumed discoloration program to one provisional regional envelope within a broader program.** Its 15 set-qualified clusters / 128 flags are about 15% of the screening list; the other 118 / 707 remain outside. These are screening counts, not confirmed defect totals or unique geographic windows. The W3 envelope includes Algiers pilot evidence as well as the intended Spain/Iberia context; that evidence cannot confirm the photographed Spain bounds.

Retain W3's prior screenshot/exact-bounds confirmation gate before building the named full W3 rectangle. The newly confirmed instruction to investigate all discoloration expands the investigation; it does not establish the screenshot extent or require rebuilding every descendant in W3. Individually confirmed defects within W3 may be proposed under the new footprint/dependency policy with explicit bounds and provenance, without pretending the entire W3 window has been validated. Track the photographed band/fill issue separately from the hue classifier so an HSV false negative cannot silently drop it. If a coherent W3 reconstruction remains warranted after calibration and geography confirmation, apply the earlier window and extension rules; otherwise submit the smaller evidence-backed scopes in the new plan.

The next deliverable should therefore be a pinned calibration/adjudication report, a stage-wise cause comparison for the representative cohort, and a first-batch plan listing source footprints, dependency keys, provenance coverage, measured/projected complete bytes and QA views. That is the evidence needed for a concrete build decision. This consult leaves the existing source documents and all deployment state unchanged.
