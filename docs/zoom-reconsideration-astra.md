# Zoom reconsideration after the iPhone experiment

2026-11-21. Design review only. Routing: direct reconsideration in the assigned Astra lane. No code changes, commits, or deployments.

**Recommendation: proceed with the stated changes.** Arm B is rejected as the solution. Proceed with a bounded Stage 2 asset and coverage pilot, with actual page-byte and portrait-footprint feasibility as explicit exit gates before global expansion. Retain persistent final-detail residency as the hypothesis, not as a demonstrated winner. Keep the existing latency and resource gates; repair their measurement before accepting any implementation.

## Evidence and limits

Sources: [original design](zoom-diagnosis-astra.md), [baseline report](zoom-stage1-evidence/phone-baseline-2026-11-21-report.txt), [Arm B report](zoom-stage1-evidence/phone-warm-arm-2026-11-21-report.txt), raw `trace-baseline.json` and `trace-warm.json`, and the current `surface.js`, `warm-ladder.js`, `main.js`, and `scripts/sat/zoom-trace-report.mjs`. All observations below are from this checkout and the supplied phone captures. Proposed limits are decisions; forecasts are explicitly estimates.

**Measured:** baseline substantial segments show 9.6 to 15.8 seconds of tile-fetch busy time, 0.9 to 2.7 seconds decode, and 1.0 to 1.6 seconds bundle time. These are per-stage unions of overlapping spans, not additive critical-path attribution. Picker and mip submission spans are small; this does not measure GPU completion. Baseline has 4 to 9 builds per substantial segment. Arm B's three substantial rounds show 706/835/738 ms report readiness, 12.0 to 19.1 seconds tile-fetch busy time, and 4 to 10 builds. Its 32 to 36 scheduled tiles land only 348 to 751 decimal KB, with 69%/77%/51% counted as used. More bytes landing does not establish complete deep-view coverage or GPU readiness.

The buffers both contain 20,000 events and start mid-round. Baseline report round 0 is partial, rounds 2/3/4 are bounded by subsequent round starts, and round 5 is a short trailing fragment with no ready window. Warm round 0 is partial; rounds 2/3/4 are the three substantive rounds, with the final one ending at export. Report labels are generated segment labels, not recovered game round identities. Neither sample supports population p95 estimates or a causal baseline-versus-warm comparison across different locations.

Three measurement qualifications materially affect the reconsideration:

1. `bytesByPool` includes `cacheTier: lru` bodies. The brief's roughly 5 to 14 MB is useful as loader byte activity, but is not all downloaded traffic. A raw audit, summing only network-tagged body completions plus successful bundle member bytes, gives the table below. The network tag means the fetch path, which may still hit SW or HTTP cache. Headers, failed/aborted partial bodies, bundle framing, and origin fan-out remain unmeasured. These are observed payload totals, not wire or billable egress measurements.
2. `timeFromLastInputTo` returns the first matching ready event after any input, using the latest input seen up to that event. It does not find the round's final gesture and final pose. `frame-presented` does not certify fade completion or intended source coverage. Accept the orchestrator's rejection of Arm B; do not claim these reports constitute a correctly instrumented p95 Tsharp test. They show no evidence that Arm B satisfies either absolute gate.
3. Abort counts are marks, not unique cancelled requests. `startInsetWindow` can emit `window-superseded` and the abort listener then emit `aborted` for the same fetch. For baseline rounds 2/3/4, the reported 251/71/95 marks comprise 197/59/63 listener marks plus 54/12/32 supersession marks. Lost bytes are unknown. Likewise, a speculative cache hit is not proof that its pixels were ultimately presented.

## 1. Page design and revised parameters

**Verdict: retain geometry, zoom set, and hard budgets provisionally; withdraw the typical-round prediction and recompute the old-path traffic forecast. Make page-byte distribution and actual footprint fit Stage 2 prerequisites.** The traces contradict the representativeness of 33 to 55 requests and roughly 0.5 to 1.1 MiB of old-path imagery per round. They do not measure encoded page size. Repeated decoding, cache reads, descendants, missing sources, and superseded footprints cannot be converted into unique page inventory by a traffic multiplier.

The original document did not demonstrate a general 0.5 to 1.6 MB page round: its roughly 520 KiB figure was an old Tokyo tile-path estimate, its typical page preparation estimate was 1.56 MiB, and its representative cold footprint estimate was 1.25 to 2.81 MiB before context. All remain estimates, not observations. The narrow original probes were inadequate for forecasting full phone rounds.

The 20 KiB successful-tile assumption is not contradicted by a large total byte count. Raw positive fetch-path body means in bounded baseline rounds are approximately 13.4/19.6/10.6 KiB, while misses and cancelled starts inflate request counts. These mixed-zoom, nonuniform samples do not predict z9 page encoding, gutters, or fallback compression. The 320 KiB page estimate and therefore 5.33 GiB storage estimate were derived from 16 tiles times an assumed tile size, not from the 33 to 55 request workload. Neither should be multiplied by the observed request inflation.

**Recomputed estimates for traffic planning:** keeping the original scenario of five rounds/day at 10,000/100,000 DAU gives 50,000/500,000 rounds/day. At 624 to 996 starts/round, this implies 31.2 to 49.8 million / 312 to 498 million browser starts/day, replacing 1.65 to 2.75 million / 16.5 to 27.5 million. At observed completed fetch-path payload of 4.499 to 8.817 decimal MB/round, the corresponding payload scenario is approximately 210 to 411 / 2,095 to 4,106 GiB/day. These are workload extrapolations, not measured egress or billing forecasts. Do not add the old 135-request Daily worst case: these traces have zero Daily-pool requests.

The page forecast still has no measured replacement: **conditional estimates** at five pages and 320 KiB/page remain 250,000/2.5 million GETs and approximately 76/763 GiB/day. At the hard speculative cap, bytes are bounded at approximately 146/1,465 GiB/day for these scenarios, excluding visible exploration. Replace five pages with actual per-round page counts before claiming savings. The original 1% to 10% origin-miss scenario remains an unvalidated assumption, not a result of these browser traces.

**Feasibility estimates:** 3 MiB needs at least 2.52 seconds of payload transfer at 10 Mbit/s, leaving part of the five-second dwell for queueing, decode, upload, and contention. At 2 Mbit/s it needs 12.58 seconds. Raising the cap to the brief's 5 to 14 decimal MB would require 4 to 11.2 seconds even at 10 Mbit/s before overhead. The phone's high old-path traffic is a reason to remove waste, not permission to enlarge preparation. Save Data remains zero speculative bytes.

## 2. Cheaper fixes

**Verdict: several deserve controlled measurement, but none is established to meet the gates as a standalone fix. Arm B's failure rejects this implementation of compressed ladder warming, not every possible prefetch policy.**

**Throttle or re-aim ordinary prefetch.** `prepareInsetPrefetch` expands current tiles into next-rung descendants, including margins; the prioritized branch actually uses the arriving visible rectangle despite its focal-3×3 comment. Hundreds of prefetch starts and numerous 404s make final-footprint targeting, source-key deduplication, a coverage index, and strict byte priority credible savings. Arm B adds a small independent focal ladder without removing this machinery, so it is not a clean test of replacement targeting. Nevertheless blob warming still leaves decode, upload, window replacement, and fade after input. It cannot certify the prepared gate's zero required post-input fetch/decode/upload contract. A cheap comparison may test these policies, but no 100/300 ms pass is demonstrated; do not defer the bounded asset pilot indefinitely for scheduler tuning.

**Abort discipline and persistent reuse.** Multiple builds and listener-confirmed cancellations support keeping useful requests across camera changes and cancelling only irrelevant work. The traces do not measure transferred bytes lost, so no reclaimed-millisecond estimate is justified. Merely finishing requests or retaining compressed blobs leaves decoded imagery disposable. It may improve cold latency, but cannot by itself meet the prepared zero-work gate. A persistent GPU tile cache with direct final-z9 preparation remains a viable alternative to pages, particularly if page overfetch fails. That is a real ownership/rendering change, not an abort flag fix; compare it at the same coverage and byte limits before selecting it as fallback.

**Bundle behavior.** The measured bundle busy unions are substantial enough to investigate, but overlap fetch/decode and cannot simply be subtracted from latency. Current code waits for the entire body and exact member set; failed bundles fall back to tiles, and window changes cancel the aggregate. Deadline handling, known-absence exclusion, stable grouping, or streaming could shorten tails. None supplies already decoded and uploaded final pixels. Even perfect bundle transport leaves post-input work in the current architecture, so no standalone prepared-gate pass is supported. Stage 2 should remove request-time assembly from the page path and measure whole page bodies, while retaining bundle diagnostics for the baseline comparison.

**Settle, picker, and mip tuning.** Small measured picker/mip CPU spans do not justify optimizing them as the primary remedy. Scheduling delays and GPU costs still require measurement, but shorter waits cannot create missing native pixels or resident textures. This is not a demonstrated route through either gate.

## 3. Gates and exact scoring

**Verdict: keep 100 ms prepared and 300 ms whole-experience limits, with their original cohort and quality definitions. Tighten instrumentation, not the limits.** The poor ready-window coverage makes replacing Tsharp with ready particularly misleading: baseline z8 windows reach only 33% to 35% native and warm z9 reaches 15%.

Tsharp remains the first presented frame at the intended final pose with at least 99% of available intended imagery coverage, introduction fade finished, and no visible quality regression over the next 500 ms. Backdate the timestamp to that first frame once stability is verified; do not add the confirmation interval to latency. Measure central 50% and full view separately; full view determines acceptance. Report camera-settle time separately without subtracting it to improve the user-facing latency. An already sharp resident view has zero imagery wait; do not require a new window-ready event.

Define available intended imagery from the versioned source manifest, not whatever happened to download. Native z9 must be used where approved and available; declared z8/z7/z5/base fallback is legitimate only where provenance says it is the intended terminal source. Report native and fallback area fractions separately, with fixed-pose visual comparisons. A transient failure or deliberately missing page remains an availability/quality failure for its intended pixels, even if fallback prevents permanent loading. Never retroactively lower the reference quality to turn 15% native into success. Ocean whose declared terminal source is the ready base must stabilize within one rendered frame.

Keep the prepared cohort: five-second clue dwell on profile B (50 ms RTT, 10 Mbit/s down, 1 Mbit/s up), complete preparation, p95 last-input-to-Tsharp ≤100 ms and median ≤50 ms, with zero post-input fetch/decode/upload required by the footprint. At least 95% of cap-fitting reference-phone rounds must finish preparation within the dwell. Required means dependency-linked work, including a fetch begun before input but still needed afterward. Background unrelated work is separately logged, not used to excuse a missing dependency.

Keep the whole-experience cohort: the same five-second-dwell workload including incomplete preparation, p95 ≤300 ms. Report all over-cap, unknown-coordinate, evicted, and context-lost rounds explicitly; do not silently omit them from the applicable experience denominator. Report immediate cold and Save Data pathways separately rather than mixing cache states to improve p95. Preserve the original cold-profile-B ≤3 s and ≥50% improvement, profile-C no-regression, frame-time, visual, reuse, and memory gates. Preserve speculative ≤3 MiB/round, median unused speculative ≤1 MiB/round, and full-round imagery bytes ≤2× paired baseline. For the latter, use consistently measured transfer bytes, not LRU byte activity, in both arms.

Repair future trace collection with gesture IDs, final camera pose, intended source coverage per frame, preparation completeness, byte-role accounting, unique request IDs, and per-round export before ring wrap. Count uncompleted sharpness as failures or censored samples with explicit bounds, never successful zeroes. Use the original 30 paired repetitions per principal site/profile, interleaved order, sample counts and p95; the present few rounds cannot replace this validation. Owner approval at matched ground scale remains necessary.

## 4. Stage 2 pilot and stopping rules

**Verdict: preserve the bounded asset-pilot scope, adding explicit byte/footprint feasibility and provenance checks before expanding it.** Stage 2 builds evidence for the later GPU/store experiment; asset generation alone cannot pass the phone residency gates.

Retain the proof sites: Tokyo, Madrid inside the pilot, a pilot boundary, Congo, Great Lakes, an atoll, ordinary land without z10, z9 holes, open ocean, antimeridian, and high latitude. Exercise portrait and landscape at target maximum depth plus margin, page corners and edges, and the large portrait rectangles exposed by these traces. Do not infer which named site corresponds to a trace segment without coordinates. Use paired controlled replays for comparison. Preserve zoom reversal, small and off-target pans, revisits, round changes during loading, background/foreground, and context restoration in later phone validation.

The coverage manifest must keep version/hash, URLs, exact encoded byte lengths, dimensions, geographic bounds, existence, and the 16-cell actual source-level map. Resolve missing z9 offline using approved z8/z7/z5/base priority; explicitly encode base-equivalent ocean and absent pages. Verify wrap and two-pixel neighbor gutters. Pilot z10 stays optional with independently identified improvement cells. Source provenance must be sufficient to compare presented pixel coverage to intended quality; a cell-level flag must not disguise a partially missing source. Publish the manifest only after referenced objects validate, and verify deterministic builds and readback hashes.

Stage 2 must measure actual page-byte distributions by zoom, terrain, and fallback class; sum bytes for each exact target footprint, context included; quantify overfetch and page counts across alignments; and calculate how many complete footprints fit both 3 MiB and GPU residency after visible/context allocations. Include abandoned preparation and off-target exploration in the workload plan. Report input reads, cache reuse, output storage, upload/readback operations, and manifest size. Reconcile counters before scaling. No world build is authorized by this memo.

Proceed to the bounded renderer pilot only if visual/source fidelity, gutters, manifest correctness, and measured footprint budgets are credible. Then measure on Sean's iPhone decode and upload cost per page, actual texture allocation and eviction, five-second preparation rate, dependency-linked Tsharp, full-round transfer, unused preparation, and frames over a 20-minute mixed session. Retain the older-iPhone, Android, and hardware-desktop checks from the original plan; software rendering cannot establish phone speed.

**Stop page expansion** if representative required footprints cannot fit the caps often enough to satisfy the unchanged whole-experience gate, if required quality needs bytes that make the cold gate unattainable, or if gutters, provenance, or source fidelity fail. Do not enlarge the budgets or lower reference quality after seeing the result. A page-overfetch failure triggers review of direct final-z9 persistent GPU tiles, not an automatic global page build. **Stop rollout** if the renderer pilot fails any mandatory latency, preparation, motion, memory, bytes, visual, or reuse gate. If a cheaper controlled arm actually passes every gate, stop page expansion and select that result for review. If neither approach passes within the constraints, stop the redesign program and retain the current default while reporting the failed requirement.

Final recommendation: **proceed with the stated changes**. The phone data strengthens the case for eliminating speculative fan-out, missing-source discovery, and disposable decoded windows. It does not prove that 4×4 pages win. Stage 2 should now answer the narrower decisive question: can real, provenance-correct pages cover the actual phone footprints within the existing budgets, before committing to the new renderer or global inventory?
