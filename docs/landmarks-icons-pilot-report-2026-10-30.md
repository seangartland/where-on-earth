## Method notes

- Coordinates: Wikidata P625 for each site's own entity, fetched live 2026-10-30, rounded to 4 dp (bank style). Five QIDs I first recalled were wrong (they resolved to people and concepts) and were corrected by entity search before any coordinate was taken; the pin audit then independently resolved the same QIDs for 10 of 11 (Lalibela differs, see its note).
- Difficulty: adapted v2. The frozen reference data (`cities15000.txt`, `k-proposed.json`) exists only under `demo/scripts/difficulty/v2-data/`, not in this worktree, so I ran a faithful reimplementation of `score_v2.py`'s math (same GeoNames population centres, same frozen cuts [4.7, 7.0, 9.6, 15.2, 24.3, 44.5, 78.9, 112.9, 201.7], same ferry-bridge table) from that directory. Country familiarity factor K = 0 for all eight countries (none appear in the K table). Pageviews: Wikimedia REST pageviews API, article level, trailing 12 complete months (2025-10 to 2026-09), daily mean and median both computed; mean/median ratios all ≤ 1.17, so no unstable-fame flags beyond the two spike rescores noted.
- Photos: Commons candidates surveyed per site with license and author from file-page extmetadata; none of the 12 is its Wikipedia article's page image. Every chosen photo was downloaded, viewed, and judged as a center square crop; all survive. Copies of the 11 used photos are in `.batch-icons-pilot/`. Stored URLs are the clean 500px `thumb.wikimedia.org` form (the API's utm tracking params stripped; 155 older bank entries carry them, left as found).
- Pin audit: `python3 scripts/pin-audit.py --locations assets/locations-landmarks-sol.json --ids <11 ids> --reverse`. GATE: 0 flagged at ≥ 3 km from every reference; all 11 risk class D. Reverse geocodes all resolve to the correct site (Eiffel Tower: 5 Avenue Anatole France; Hagia Sophia: Ayasofya Meydanı; Notre-Dame: John Paul II Square forecourt; Rushmore: Mount Rushmore National Memorial, Keystone SD; Golden Gate: the bridge itself).

## Clues and facts as stored (verbatim)

### eiffel-tower
- clue: Eiffel Tower, France 🇫🇷
- fact: Completed in 1889 as the entrance arch to the Paris World's Fair, it was meant to be dismantled after 20 years; it survived because the French military found the wireless aerials on its summit too useful to lose.
- image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Eiffel_tower_from_trocadero.jpg/500px-Eiffel_tower_from_trocadero.jpg

### statue-of-liberty
- clue: Statue of Liberty, United States 🇺🇸
- fact: Its copper skin is just 2.4 millimetres thick, about two stacked pennies, hung on an iron skeleton designed by Gustave Eiffel; the metal arrived from Paris a dull brown and oxidized to its present green within 30 years.
- image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Statue_of_Liberty_National_Monument_STLI2254.jpg/500px-Statue_of_Liberty_National_Monument_STLI2254.jpg

### golden-gate-bridge
- clue: Golden Gate Bridge, United States 🇺🇸
- fact: Its paint is a custom shade called International Orange, chosen to stay visible in San Francisco's fog; when it opened in 1937 its 1,280-metre main span was the longest suspension span in the world.
- image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Golden_Gate_Bridge_as_seen_from_Marshall%E2%80%99s_Beach%2C_March_2018.jpg/500px-Golden_Gate_Bridge_as_seen_from_Marshall%E2%80%99s_Beach%2C_March_2018.jpg

### sydney-opera-house
- clue: Sydney Opera House, Australia 🇦🇺
- fact: Every white shell 'sail' is a rib cut from the surface of a single sphere, the trick Jørn Utzon found in 1961 to make the shapes buildable; the final bill came to A$102 million against a A$7 million estimate.
- image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Sydney_Opera_House_01.jpg/500px-Sydney_Opera_House_01.jpg

### hagia-sophia
- clue: Hagia Sophia, Turkey 🇹🇷
- fact: Raised in just five years and completed in 537 under Justinian I, its dome spans 31 metres, and the building stood as the world's largest cathedral for nearly a thousand years.
- image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Hagia_Sophia_Mars_2013.jpg/500px-Hagia_Sophia_Mars_2013.jpg

### leaning-tower-of-pisa
- clue: Leaning Tower of Pisa, Italy 🇮🇹
- fact: It began tilting before the third storey was finished in the 1170s, because one side of the foundation sits on soft clay; a rescue in the 1990s pulled the lean back by about 44 centimetres and left it stable for at least 200 years.
- image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/The_Leaning_Tower_of_Pisa_SB.jpeg/500px-The_Leaning_Tower_of_Pisa_SB.jpeg

### notre-dame-de-paris
- clue: Notre-Dame de Paris, France 🇫🇷
- fact: Its 96-metre spire, added by Viollet-le-Duc in 1859, crashed through the burning roof in the fire of April 2019; the cathedral reopened in December 2024 with the spire rebuilt to the same design, 861 years after building began.
- image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Paris_Notre-Dame_Southeast_View_01.JPG/500px-Paris_Notre-Dame_Southeast_View_01.JPG

### neuschwanstein-castle
- clue: Neuschwanstein Castle, Germany 🇩🇪
- fact: Ludwig II of Bavaria began this medieval fantasy in 1868 with flush toilets, central heating and telephone lines hidden inside; he slept there only 172 nights before his death in 1886, and the castle later modelled Disney's Sleeping Beauty Castle.
- image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Neuschwanstein_Castle_2024-02.jpg/500px-Neuschwanstein_Castle_2024-02.jpg

### nazca-lines
- clue: Nazca Lines, Peru 🇵🇪
- fact: The Nazca culture scraped these figures into the desert floor between about 500 BC and AD 500 by clearing dark stones to expose the pale earth beneath; the largest figure, a pelican, stretches 285 metres, and the lines went unnoticed until aircraft flew over them in the 1920s.
- image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a5/L%C3%ADneas_de_Nazca%2C_Nazca%2C_Per%C3%BA%2C_2015-07-29%2C_DD_52.JPG/500px-L%C3%ADneas_de_Nazca%2C_Nazca%2C_Per%C3%BA%2C_2015-07-29%2C_DD_52.JPG

### lalibela
- clue: Lalibela, Ethiopia 🇪🇹
- fact: Its eleven medieval churches were not built up but carved down into solid volcanic tuff under King Lalibela in the late 12th century; the last and best preserved, Bete Giyorgis, is hewn in the shape of a cross and reached only through a tunnel cut in the rock.
- image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Bete_Giyorgis_01.jpg/500px-Bete_Giyorgis_01.jpg

### mount-rushmore
- clue: Mount Rushmore, United States 🇺🇸
- fact: Gutzon Borglum and 400 workers carved the four 18-metre faces between 1927 and 1941, blasting away some 410,000 tons of Black Hills granite with dynamite, then smoothing the stone with drills in a pattern called honeycombing.
- image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Dean_Franklin_-_06.04.03_Mount_Rushmore_Monument_%28by-sa%29.jpg/500px-Dean_Franklin_-_06.04.03_Mount_Rushmore_Monument_%28by-sa%29.jpg
